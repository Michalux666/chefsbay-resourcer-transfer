# The role-scoped second look at people rejected after an unlock

Audience: the owner first, then the operator and whoever maintains the code. Decisions: `docs/DECISIONS.md` section 17 (RS-1 to RS-15). Install note: `docs/UPDATE-E.md`. Limits: `docs/KNOWN-LIMITS.md` section 12 (K-RS1 to K-RS12). Settings: `docs/ENV.md` (`CV_RESURFACE`, `CV_RESURFACE_MAX_PER_DAY`, `CV_RESURFACE_MIN_CREDITS`).

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

When CV screening is on (`CV_SCREEN=on`), a person who was unlocked and then rejected by the CV stage for ONE role is no longer lost for every other role. If the same person comes up later in a search for a DIFFERENT job title, they are screened again as normal: the snippet screening decides for the new role and, if it approves, the contact details and the CV are fetched again, the CV stage makes a fresh decision for the new role, and the person is pushed to Zoho once or rejected again, for that role. For the SAME role the first rejection stays final, a person who was pushed to Zoho is never looked at again, and nobody is ever pushed twice. The screening of the new search IS the classification of the person: no profile of a rejected person is stored, and no CV or contact detail is kept (the CV is downloaded again when needed and deleted after processing).

## 2. Why

Before the unlock a rejection is tied to the job title (`candidate_rejections`): someone poor for a Chef search may be right for a Kitchen Porter search, and the pipeline reconsiders them. After the unlock it was not: the person stayed marked as unlocked in `candidates.db`, and the search filter skipped every unlocked person for every role ("we already hold their CV", decision CVS-8). But after a CV rejection the CV is deleted, so "we already hold their CV" was no longer true, and someone over-qualified or in the wrong specialty for one role was lost for all of them. On 2026-10-01 the owner decided (RS-1 to RS-4): the rejection is role-scoped like the snippet rejection; a second Caterer credit (or Reed profile view) for the re-download is accepted; the feature is on by default; and a role is never charged twice.

## 3. The rule, exactly as built

Where each fact is read is in brackets. The rule is the same for Caterer and Reed and lives in `scripts/lib/resurface.js` (`classify`), called by `candidates-db.js` (`check-batch-scoped` for Caterer, `resurfaceBatchReed` for Reed). An unlocked candidate is NOT skipped for the current search title R when ALL of these hold:

- (a) the candidate has no Zoho id and was never pushed [`candidates.zoho_id` empty and `candidates.zoho_pushed_at` empty; and no results file of Phase 2 records a push for the id (`downloads/phase2-results-*.json`, a row with status `new` or `duplicate` and a Zoho id)];
- (b) the candidate has a RECORD OF AN UNLOCK-AND-REJECT among its `candidate_rejections` rows [`caterer_id` or `reed_id`]: a CV rejection (origin `cv:...`), a row of this feature (origin `resurface:...`, which includes `resurface:postunlock`, the role Phase 1's own review rejected after an unlock), or, for Caterer only, a legacy row whose title is the sentinel `*` or empty (role unknown). A plain pre-unlock rejection of another role (a real title, origin `pipeline` or `log-backfill`) is NOT such a record: it says nothing about the role the candidate was unlocked for, and a candidate unlocked for role Y whose push failed, with an earlier snippet rejection for X, would otherwise be charged a second time for Y (K-RS3);
- (c) no `candidate_rejections` row for R exists, of any origin, including this feature's own `resurface:started` [the same table, `job_title` compared exactly, as the old scoped rule does];
- (d) the daily cap allows one more [`runtime/cv-resurface.json` today's `started` against `CV_RESURFACE_MAX_PER_DAY`]; the credit reserve is checked by Phase 1 right before the claim (section 4);
- (e) `CV_RESURFACE` is on and `CV_SCREEN` is on [profile `.env`, read through `scripts/lib/env.js`; without `CV_SCREEN=on` no CV rejection exists, so the rule can never fire];

and nothing of the candidate is in flight [a `candidate-<id>.json` or a CV file in `downloads/`, or an entry of a queue (`approved-queue-*`, `merged-queue-*`, `reed-approved-queue-*`) whose Phase 2 has not finished: a second charge must not start while the first outcome is open].

Everything else keeps the old skip. In detail:

- The unknown-title sentinel `*` blocks every title for a candidate who is NOT unlocked, exactly as today (a pre-unlock rejection, C4). Only for an unlocked, never-pushed candidate is `*` read as "role unknown" (it neither blocks nor counts as the role of the earlier unlock).
- An unlocked, never-pushed candidate with NO record of an unlock-and-reject (no row at all, or only a plain pre-unlock rejection of another role: a stranded or failed processing state, or a rejection after an unlock by the old system, section 7) keeps the old skip: the stranded recovery owns it.
- Reed has no `unlocked` flag that the pipeline sets (it is always 0, even for pushed people), so the Reed rule reads the rows only: a Reed candidate needs a `cv:` or `resurface:` row (the old system wrote no Reed rows, and a Reed snippet rejection writes none, so those stay permanent: K-RS7).
- Case: job titles are compared exactly (K-RS8).
- A failure anywhere (a missing table, an unreadable database, an unreadable counter file) reads as "not eligible": the old skip.
- A candidate whose second look FAILED today without a charge (the platform refused the unlock and the balance did not move) is not looked at again before the next London day (section 4, step 4), so a refusing platform cannot make every run screen the same people again.

## 4. What happens, step by step

Caterer (the unlock is in Phase 1, the CV in Phase 2):

1. Phase 1 `dedupe.js` asks `check-batch-scoped`; the answer adds `resurface` (the eligible ids, at most as many as the cap leaves) and `resurfaceCapped` (how many the cap held back; they stay in `inDb`, skipped). With the switch off the answer is exactly `{"inDb":[...]}` as before. The card is flagged and the log says `RESURFACE: unlocked earlier and rejected for another role - screened again for this role`. If anyone is flagged, the credit reserve is checked right there, BEFORE the screening (the balance is read once; unreadable or below `CV_RESURFACE_MIN_CREDITS` is remembered for the rest of the run): the flagged people are then left skipped, not screened (no Jev call is spent on people who cannot be re-opened), nothing recorded, and a broken balance reader costs one read, not one per candidate.
2. The normal snippet screening decides for the new role. A rejection is recorded for the new title like any rejection (`reject-title`), so the person is not screened for that role again.
3. If approved, `unlock.js` calls `phase1/resurface.js begin()` before the unlock. The resurfaced cards of a page are unlocked AFTER the normal cards of that page (the balance read navigates the shared browser to the search page, and the normal unlocks must not depend on where the browser is: K-RS10). `begin()` takes the reserve check again (the reading of the dedupe step is reused while no normal unlock has spent a credit since); then the claim is written (`resurface-claim`): a `candidate_rejections` row for the new title, origin `resurface:started`, atomically and durably, taking one slot of the cap. Only then is the unlock endpoint called.
4. After the call the balance is read again and the cost is measured (charged, not charged, unknown). An unlock that failed and spent nothing gives the claim back and the candidate is noted for the day (not tried again before the next London day, not counted toward the run-stopping streak of unlock failures); in every other case (it worked, it was charged, or the cost cannot be told) the claim stays. A candidate Phase 1's own review rejects right after the unlock is counted, and while the feature is active that rejection is recorded for the role (origin `resurface:postunlock`), so the person is not screened or charged again for it. The queue entry carries `resurfaced: true`, `resurfaceCharge` and `resurfaceCredits`; `phase1Stats.resurfaced` counts them, including the re-opens that never reached the queue (`notQueued`: rejected right after the unlock, or failed after a charge), so every charge is in the block.
5. Phase 2 downloads the CV like any other, the CV stage decides for this role with a fresh Jev decision, then: pushed (create, attach, delete the CV; the claim row becomes `resurface:pushed`) or rejected again (the claim row becomes `cv:<codes>`, the CV and the JSON are deleted; the row of the earlier role stays). The existing duplicate checks are unchanged and apply: a candidate that already has a Zoho id is skipped before the push, and a duplicate answer from Zoho never creates a second record.

Reed (the profile view and the CV come from one download call, in Phase 2):

1. `reed-phase1.js` asks `resurfaceBatchReed` for each page; the eligible candidates are screened again for this role and, if approved, queued with `resurfaced: true`. A resurfaced Reed candidate the snippet screening rejects gets a row `resurface:snippet` for this role (Reed keeps no row for any other rejection).
2. Phase 2 downloads the resurfaced Reed candidates ALONE, after the others, so the change of the day's profile views is attributable to one candidate: the views left today are read (none left, or unreadable, means held back); the claim is written; the download runs; the views are read again and the cost is measured. A download that fails is not pushed as a bare card; one that cost nothing gives the claim back.

## 5. What it costs, how it is shown, how it is capped

Whether Caterer and Reed charge a second credit or view for re-opening a person who is already unlocked cannot be known offline (K-RS1). The owner expects both to and accepts it, so there is no kill switch on a charge. Instead:

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Per run: resurfaced candidates, charged, not charged, charge unknown, credits spent (also for people rejected right after the unlock), Reed views spent, pushed, rejected again, held back, stopped by the cap, by the reserve, balance unreadable | the `resurfaced` block of the Phase 2 results file; the same numbers per source in `catererStats.resurfaced` and `reedStats.resurfaced`, which travel in the `caterer_json` and `reed_json` columns of `run_results` (no schema change); one line in the Phase 2 output |
| Per day and over a period | `node scripts/cv-report.js --days 7`, block RESURFACED (also in `--json`), from `runtime/cv-resurface.json` |
| Every evening | one line in the 18:00 digest (`node scripts/alerts-deliver.js --dry-run --digest`), only on a day it did something |
| A cap or the reserve held someone back | the WARN alert `cv-resurface-cap-reached`, once a day |

How a charge is measured: the Caterer balance is read right before and right after the unlock call (the call that returns the contact details and the CV link); the Reed measure is the change of the day's `reed_daily_usage.profile_views` around the download (read strictly: a counter that cannot be read is "unknown", never "no views"). If a balance cannot be read the candidate is counted as "charged unknown" and the report says so. A resumed Phase 1 run (after a crash) carries only the re-opens that reached its queue in its results block; the daily counters (`cv-report.js`, the digest) are exact. The Caterer CV download of Phase 2 is not measured (K-RS6).

Runaway protection (design defaults, not yet confirmed by the owner): at most 40 candidates a London day, Caterer and Reed together (`CV_RESURFACE_MAX_PER_DAY`); no candidate is looked at again while the Caterer balance is below 1000 credits (`CV_RESURFACE_MIN_CREDITS`; the normal unlock path has no reserve, so this one is new); for Reed the daily limit of profile views. A candidate that a cap stops simply stays skipped for now: not rejected, nothing recorded against them, eligible again the next day or when the balance is above the reserve. The reserve and the cap raise their own WARN alert, each once a London day. At the cap the extra cost is at most 40 credits or views a day, about a fifth of the 181 CVs a day the target needs.

Never twice for the same role (RS-4): the claim row for the new title is written BEFORE the person is fetched again, in one atomic statement, so a crash, a retry, an overlapping run, a queue recovered after a hold and a second territory all find it. A role the earlier unlock did not record (the old system) is charged once for any role, which records its role; a further new role is still fine, with no limit on the number of distinct roles.

## 6. What the owner can change

| Setting (profile `.env`, `hermes -p resourcer config set ...`) | Default | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `CV_RESURFACE` | `on` | `on` (also `true`, `1`, `yes`) or `off` (also `false`, `0`, `no`). `off` restores the old rule exactly (every unlocked person is skipped for every role). Anything else is a typo and is read as `off`, with a warning line in every Phase 1 log and in `cv-report.js`. Effective only while `CV_SCREEN` is `on`: with `shadow` or `off` there are no CV rejections and the rule never fires. Say it plainly: with the shipped `CV_SCREEN=shadow` this whole feature does nothing |
| `CV_RESURFACE_MAX_PER_DAY` | `40` | the daily cap; `0` is the same as off |
| `CV_RESURFACE_MIN_CREDITS` | `1000` | the credit reserve |

No restart is needed: every Phase 1 and Phase 2 process reads them. To undo what the feature recorded: nothing needs undoing (a row `resurface:*` is a normal rejection for that title; the old code treats it as one); to let one person be screened for one title again, delete that row.

## 7. What the old system left in the database, and what the rule recognises

The old system recorded a rejection AFTER an unlock like this: after the post-unlock review it called `candidates-db.js add <id>` (the candidate stays `unlocked = 1`) and never `reject-title`, so there is NO `candidate_rejections` row, no title, and no Zoho id. Rows of the old system exist only for rejections BEFORE the unlock (origin `pipeline`, since 2026-08-03, with the job title of the search) and the 6,781 rows rebuilt from console logs (origin `log-backfill`, dated 2026-08-03; a title, or the sentinel `*` where the title could not be known). In the old database about 1,336 Caterer candidates are unlocked without a Zoho id: post-unlock rejections (about 1,100) and failed pushes, which cannot be told apart.

So the rule recognises exactly what the rows say about an UNLOCK and a REJECTION: an unlocked, never-pushed candidate with a legacy row whose title is the sentinel `*` (or empty) is eligible for any title, as role unknown: one second charge, which records its role (the claim row), after which the same role is never charged again and a further new role is still fine. A legacy row with a REAL title and origin `pipeline` or `log-backfill` is a rejection BEFORE an unlock as far as anyone can tell (or, for a person unlocked for another role whose push failed, a stranded state): it is no record of an unlock-and-reject, so that person keeps the old skip (K-RS3). A legacy post-unlock rejection with no row at all keeps the old skip too: it cannot be told from a stranded state, and the brief says a candidate with no record keeps the old skip. So most of the roughly 1,100 legacy post-unlock rejections are NOT freed by this release; only the ones that happen to carry a `*` or empty-title row are. How many that is cannot be counted offline; the `started` count of the first days shows it, and a read-only count (unlocked, no Zoho id, no row, non-empty `candidates.role`) would show whether the old data holds a role for the others. Recovering the rest needs a dated list from the old console logs (the old `log-backfill` method) and an owner decision.

Going forward the new system closes the gap it would otherwise recreate: with the feature active, a Phase 1 post-unlock rejection is recorded for its role (`resurface:postunlock`), and a CV rejection always was (`cv:...`).

## 8. What the real code does for an already unlocked card (the trace)

- Where the platform state is read: the card in the search results. `scripts/extract-js.b64` marks a card `unlockedPrev` when its HTML says "Unlocked previously" and `neverUnlocked` when it says "Never unlocked". The pipeline's own state is `candidates.unlocked`. The decision of the rule is made from the database; the card's flag is only logged (`the platform shows: unlocked previously` or `never unlocked`).
- What the real code does for such a card: before this feature an unlocked candidate was skipped before any screening. A card the DATABASE did not know but the platform showed as "Unlocked previously" (a person unlocked outside this pipeline) went through the same path as a new card: screened, and if approved `caterer-unlock.js` called the unlock endpoint (`UnlockCandidate` with the card's `CandidateData` token, through the signed-in browser) like for any card. The legacy system did the same for years.
- The brief asked not to call the unlock endpoint again when the platform shows the person as already unlocked, and to take the contact details and the CV "through the normal download path". That is not possible here: the unlock response is the only place this system gets the contact details, the encrypted candidate id and the audit id that the CV download needs (the card has none of them, and the CV download URL is built from them). The unlock call IS the re-open. Whether the platform charges for it is its answer, and this feature measures it (section 5). This is deviation 1 of the build.
- Where a charge could happen: (1) `UnlockCandidate` in Phase 1 (the one call documented to spend a credit; the balance shown on the search page, `.litCandidatesViewed`, falls); (2) `CandidateDownloadCV.aspx` in Phase 2 (believed free after an unlock; not measured, K-RS6); (3) Reed: the profile call and the CV call of one download in Phase 2 (`reed-download.js`; each profile call costs one daily view and is counted in `reed_daily_usage`).
- Nothing treats a second unlock as a new first unlock: `add` keeps `unlocked = 1`, the Zoho id is never touched by Phase 1, and the queue entry and the results carry their own `resurfaced` flag.

## 11. Compared with the previous release

C5 asks for byte-identical behaviour with `CV_SCREEN` shadow or off. Besides the repository test of scenario 18 (`18.9`, the new code with the switch on and off), the author compared the new code with the code of the release before it (commit `315e99b`, a pristine copy) once, outside the repository: the standard rehearsal world of scenario 2 (three result pages, a post-unlock reject, a duplicate in Zoho, a missing phone number recovered from the CV) was run on both code trees in three modes (the shipped defaults, `CV_SCREEN=shadow` and `CV_SCREEN=off`, each with the new switch at its default of on), and everything the pipeline left behind was written to one normalised file per run: the Phase 2 results files and the approved queue (without clock values), the run status numbers, the `candidates`, `candidate_rejections` and `run_results` tables, every Zoho call, the CV shadow log (decisions only), the alerts, and the files left in `downloads/` and `runtime/`. The three pairs are IDENTICAL byte for byte (182,856 bytes in the default and shadow modes, 181,563 in the off mode). A change of the previous release's behaviour would show there.

What this does not show: with `CV_SCREEN=on` the behaviour differs by design (scenario 18 is the proof of the difference, and of nothing else changing).

## 12. How the tests were checked

Mutation checks on a copy of the tree (a one-line change of the production code each, then the named test files; every mutation had to make at least one test fail, and each did): the row of the new title ignored by the rule (M1); a pushed candidate no longer excluded (M2); an unlocked candidate with no record no longer kept at the old skip (M3); the sentinel read as blocking for an unlocked candidate (M4); the rule ignoring `CV_SCREEN` (M5); the cap ignored by the classification (M6) and by the claim (M7); the sign of the measurement swapped (M8); the in-flight guard off (M9); the alert raised every time instead of once a day (M10); the claim without its eligibility check (M11); Phase 1 not writing the claim before the unlock (M12); the dedupe ignoring the answer (M13); the command always printing the two extra keys, so that the switch-off answer is no longer exactly `{"inDb":[...]}` (M14); the claim row not becoming the CV rejection in Phase 2 (M15); Reed resurfaced candidates downloaded together with the others (M16); Reed claims not written (M17). The finalizer added a second round after three independent verifiers read the build, each again a one-line change of the production code that had to fail a named test: an unlocked candidate with only a plain pre-unlock row of another role made eligible again (F1: the rule of section 3(b)); a claim given back when the unlock failed and the cost is unknown (Phase 1) and, for Reed, when the download failed after a view was spent; the reserve compared with `<=` instead of `<`; the latch of an unreadable balance removed (a read per candidate); the resurfaced cards no longer unlocked after the normal ones; the failed list of the day not consulted; the per-run totals without the re-opens that never reached the queue; the post-unlock rejection not recorded; the Reed usage reader not strict. The suites: `node --test "tests/**/*.test.js"` and `bash tests/e2e-linux.sh` (scenario 18 is new; the others are unchanged).
