# The role scope: people whose role was never recorded, Caterer and Reed

Audience: the owner first, then the operator and whoever maintains the code. Decisions: `docs/DECISIONS.md` section 18 (RL-1 to RL-17). Install note: `docs/UPDATE-F.md`. Limits: `docs/KNOWN-LIMITS.md` section 13 (K-RL1 to K-RL13) and the resolved K-RS3 and K-RS7 of section 12. Settings: `docs/ENV.md` (`ROLE_SCOPE_LEGACY`, `ROLE_SCOPE_MIN_AGE_DAYS`, `ROLE_SCOPE_REED_MAX_PER_RUN`). It builds on the role-scoped second look of `docs/RESURFACE.md` and uses the same claim.

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

A rejection applies to the role that was searched, and to nothing else. Before this release that was true for a Caterer rejection BEFORE the unlock, and for a CV rejection (when `CV_SCREEN` is `on`). It was not true for two large groups of people, and the owner said on 2026-10-01 that they must be treated the same way, even if it costs further unlocks: (a) Reed people rejected at the snippet stage, who were skipped for EVERY job title for ever (one "seen" row, no title), and (b) the people the OLD system left behind, whose CV was seen and rejected after an unlock but who carry no record of any role (Caterer: unlocked, never pushed to Zoho; Reed: seen, never pushed). From now on a new Reed rejection is recorded PER JOB TITLE, the same profile under another title is screened as normal, and under the same title it is skipped. A person whose role was never recorded is screened ONCE for the title of the search they next come up in; the outcome is recorded for that title, so the same role is never screened (or paid for) again, and a different new role is fine, with no limit on the number of roles. Nobody who is in Zoho, or in the middle of being processed, is ever touched. It works whether `CV_SCREEN` is `shadow`, `off` or `on`.

## 2. What the owner said, and what was agreed (recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md` RL-1 to RL-5)

On 2026-10-01 the owner said: "Reed/Caterer rejections where we have seen their CV but only used the keyword search should follow the same footpath of proper screening even if it means further unlocks, and these should not be blocked for all roles but only for those that we searched for." Earlier decisions that still hold:

1. A rejection applies only to the role (job title) that was searched; any new role is screened as normal.
2. A second Caterer credit (or Reed profile view) is accepted, but only for a DIFFERENT role and NEVER twice for the SAME role; any new role is fine to unlock again, with no limit on the number of distinct roles.
3. When the role of an earlier unlock or rejection was not recorded (legacy), ONE more look is accepted for any role, and that look records its role so the same role is never charged again.
4. No loop: each (candidate, role) is screened at most once.
5. These behaviours are ON by default.

The owner was told that Reed was found to violate (1) and was not fixed at once. This release fixes it (section 3, "Reed, new rejections") and does it for the old rows as well (section 3, "legacy").

## 3. The rule, exactly as built

Where each fact is read is in brackets. One function decides (`classifyDetailed` in `scripts/lib/resurface.js`), for Caterer and Reed alike; `candidates-db.js` (`check-batch-scoped`, `resurfaceBatchReed`) asks it and the claim (`claim`) asks it again at the moment of the charge. A candidate who is not in Zoho, not in flight and has NO row for the title of this search (any origin) is one of three KINDS, or none:

| Kind | Who | Needs | Charged how |
|---|---|---|---|
| `resurface` | the CV rule of `docs/RESURFACE.md` (a CV rejection, or a `resurface:` row, of another role) | `CV_RESURFACE` on and `CV_SCREEN` on | claim, then measured; counts toward the cap |
| `legacy` | the role was never recorded (this document) | `ROLE_SCOPE_LEGACY` on (default), nothing else | claim, then measured; Caterer counts toward the cap, Reed does not |
| `scoped` | Reed only: rejected or approved for ANOTHER title, recorded per title | nothing (no switch) | none: an ordinary screening |

One list, one kind per person, one claim: a person the CV rule has is never also called legacy (the CV rule wins), and nobody is listed twice.

**Caterer, `legacy`** [`candidates.unlocked = 1`, `zoho_id` empty and `zoho_pushed_at` empty; `candidate_rejections`; `candidates.created_at`; `downloads/`]. All of these hold: the person is unlocked and was never pushed to Zoho; there is no row for this title; nothing of them is in flight (no `candidate-<id>.json` or CV file in `downloads/`, no entry of a queue whose Phase 2 has not finished, no results file that records a push); and they have no CV rejection (that is the CV rule's) and EITHER are old (`created_at` empty, as in the legacy rows, or older than `ROLE_SCOPE_MIN_AGE_DAYS`, default 14 days, never below 8 because the stranded-candidate recovery owns the first 7) whatever else the rows say: no row at all, only rows whose title is `*` or empty ("role unknown"), or only PLAIN pre-unlock rejections (origin `pipeline` or `log-backfill`) of OTHER titles, OR they already had a look of this system for another role (a `resurface:` row: a role is recorded, a NEW role is fine, with no age rule). A rejection from the keyword search binds ONLY the title that was searched (the owner's own sentence, DECISIONS RL-1 and RL-15): it says nothing about the role of the unlock, so a person with such a rejection for one title is screened once for any other title, and never again for the title of the rejection. (The first build kept these people, 214 at cutover, blocked for every role; the finalisation corrected that.) A person younger than the minimum age keeps the old skip and becomes eligible by himself when he passes the age (K-RL2): this includes a new "unlocked previously" card rejected at the snippet stage for one title, which waits at most 14 days before another title screens it.

**Reed, new rejections (R-C1, no switch).** A Reed card rejected at the snippet stage gets a row for the searched title, origin `reed:snippet`, written BEFORE the seen row (a kill between the two leaves a skip for that title, never a loop). A card approved gets a row `reed:approved` for that title when the queue carrying it has been written and read back (also before its seen row). Both rows are ordinary rows of `candidate_rejections` (a `reed_id`, a title, a date, an origin): no schema change, no migration, nothing personal. The skip then reads them: a Reed person with a row for THIS title is skipped; one with rows for other titles only is `scoped` and is screened as normal; a person with no row at all is `legacy`. The seen row itself is written as before (the counts, the dashboard and everything that reads `candidates` are unchanged).

**Reed, `legacy`** [`candidates` for the `reed_id`: `unlocked` empty or 0, `zoho_id` and `zoho_pushed_at` empty; `candidate_rejections`; `downloads/`]. A seen-only row with no row at all (the 21,915 rows of the old system and of the installed release), or only `resurface:` rows of other roles (a look of this system that already happened), and nothing in flight, is let through to screening once for whatever title the search is for. A row with `unlocked = 1` (a profile viewed, which this code never sets) keeps the old skip.

**Everything else keeps the old skip**, in particular: a person in Zoho; a person in flight; the unknown-title sentinel `*` on a person who is NOT unlocked (it still blocks every title, as before); CV rejections when `CV_RESURFACE` or `CV_SCREEN=on` is off; any failure of the database or of the counters (a missing table, an unreadable file reads as "nobody", the old skip, never a crash).

### What the setting controls, and what it does not

`ROLE_SCOPE_LEGACY` (on or off; unset is on; anything else is off, with a warning in every Phase 1 log and in `cv-report.js`) controls ONE thing: whether people whose role was never recorded are let through (Caterer `legacy`, Reed `legacy`, including a person whose recorded look was of this system). With it `off` those people keep the old skip, the dedupe and every flow behave as the release before (section 11). It does NOT switch off the per-title record of NEW Reed rejections and approvals, nor the `scoped` rule: that is the fix of the violation (R-C1) and has no switch. It does not touch `CV_RESURFACE`, which keeps controlling the CV rule alone, and it does not depend on `CV_SCREEN`. A third setting, `ROLE_SCOPE_REED_MAX_PER_RUN` (default 100), bounds how many Reed people of unrecorded role ONE run lets through (section 6). With it on and nothing legacy in the database the behaviour is the release before, except for the new Reed rows (section 11).

## 4. What happens, step by step

Caterer (the unlock is in Phase 1, the CV in Phase 2):

1. Phase 1 `dedupe.js` asks `check-batch-scoped`; the answer adds `resurface` (the people to screen again, at most as many as the daily cap leaves) and `legacy` (the part of it that is the role scope), only when they exist; with the switch off and nothing eligible it is exactly `{"inDb":[...]}` as before. A legacy person is flagged and the log says `ROLE SCOPE: unlocked earlier, the role is not recorded and nothing was ever pushed - screened for this role (one more look, recorded for this role)`. The credit reserve is checked right there, before the screening, once per run, exactly as for the CV rule.
2. The normal snippet screening decides for this title. A legacy person REJECTED here is recorded for this title in the form the rule reads (origin `resurface:snippet`), counted as a look given and rejected again (no credit is spent), and is never screened for this title again; another title screens them again.
3. If approved, `unlock.js` takes the claim (a row for this title, origin `resurface:started`, atomic and durable, one slot of the daily cap) BEFORE it calls the unlock endpoint again, measures the credits around the call, and queues the person with `resurfaced: true, legacy: true` and the measured charge. An unlock that failed and spent nothing gives the claim back (the person is not tried again that London day); in every other case the claim stays.
3b. A person the review right after the unlock rejects stays recorded for the title (the claim row), counted as a look rejected again, charge included.
4. Phase 2 downloads the CV like any other. The CV stage decides for this title as always (with `CV_SCREEN=shadow` it records and never rejects). Pushed: the claim row becomes `resurface:pushed`; rejected: it becomes `cv:<codes>` (so the CV rule can later look at them for yet another title when `CV_SCREEN` is on). A person already in Zoho is skipped before the push, and a duplicate answer never creates a second record.

Reed (a profile view and the CV come from one download call, in Phase 2):

1. `reed-phase1.js` asks `resurfaceBatchReed` once per page. The skip is: a row for this title (`judged`), or a seen row that the rules above do not let through. `scoped` people are screened as normal. `legacy` people are screened once, flagged `resurfaced: true, legacy: true` in the queue; a legacy person REJECTED gets a `reed:snippet` row for this title and is counted (look given, rejected again, no view spent). Approvals that are not claimed looks get their `reed:approved` row after the queue exists.
2. Phase 2 downloads the claimed looks ALONE, after the others: the views left today are read (none left, or unreadable, holds the person back); the claim is written (the person stays skipped for the title if the process dies now); the download runs; the views are read again and the cost measured. A download that failed and cost nothing gives the claim back; one that spent keeps it.
3. The CV stage decides for this title; the claim row becomes `resurface:pushed` or `cv:<codes>`; a `reed:approved` row that the CV stage rejects becomes the CV rejection (one row per person and title).

## 5. Safety: who is "in flight", and how a seen-only row is told apart from an approved person (R-C6)

A Reed row says only "seen". An approved person is marked seen too (after the queue is written), and so is a person rejected at the snippet stage. Nothing in the row tells them apart, so the guards are the files and the rows around it. A person is NEVER let through (by any kind) when:

| Guard | Where it is read |
|---|---|
| a Zoho id is set, or `zoho_pushed_at` is set (pushed) | `candidates` |
| `unlocked = 1` for the legacy and scoped kinds (a profile or CV was viewed) | `candidates` (Reed: never set by this code; Caterer: required for the Caterer kinds) |
| a `candidate-<id>.json` or a CV file (`cv-<id>.*`, `cv-reed-<id>.*`) exists | `downloads/` |
| an entry of a queue whose Phase 2 has not finished (`approved-queue-*`, `merged-queue-*`, `reed-approved-queue-*`, finished = a results file with `completedAt`) | `downloads/` |
| a results file records a push for the id (a crash between the Zoho write and the database write) | `downloads/phase2-results-*.json` |
| a row for THIS title exists, of any origin, also an unfinished claim | `candidate_rejections` |
| the daily cap is spent (Caterer legacy, the CV rule) | `runtime/cv-resurface.json` |
| the failed list of the day names them (a refused unlock) | `runtime/cv-resurface.json` |

`reed_daily_usage` cannot tell people apart (it holds a count per day, not a list). It bounds the Reed looks instead: Phase 1 lowers the run limit to the views left today and does not screen at all when none is left; Phase 2 holds a claimed look back when no view is left.

What the guards cannot see is an approved Reed person whose Phase 2 failed long ago and whose files are gone (K-RL5): they look like any legacy person and get their one more look, which the owner accepted for people whose role is not recorded.

New approvals do NOT look like legacy people: every Reed approval has its `reed:approved` row for its title, so a new approval that failed is skipped for that title for ever (never twice for the same role), and a different title is an ordinary screening (it may cost a second view for a different role, which the owner accepted). An approval that went through a merged queue stays "in flight" until retention removes its `reed-approved-queue` file (at least 3 days), so a new title for that person waits that long (K-RL11, a safe direction).

## 6. What it costs, how it is shown, how it is capped (R-C8)

Whether Caterer and Reed charge a second credit or view for a person who was unlocked earlier cannot be known offline (K-RS1); the owner accepts it. So it is counted and reported, and capped where the platform charges per unlock:

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Per run: people screened, rejected at the snippet stage, rejected after the unlock, queued, charged, credits, Reed views, pushed, rejected by the CV stage, held back (a `legacy` sub-block of the `resurfaced` block of the Phase 2 results file; the per-source parts travel in the `caterer_json` and `reed_json` columns of `run_results`, no schema change); one line in the Phase 2 output | the results file, `run_results`, the Phase 2 log |
| Per day and over a period: looks given (Caterer, Reed), rejected again, pushed, charged, credits, views | `node scripts/cv-report.js --days 7`, block RESURFACED, line "role scope: one more look" (also in `--json`), from `runtime/cv-resurface.json` |
| Every evening | the 18:00 digest line "Resurfaced today" gets a sentence "Role scope (role never recorded, one more look): ..." on the same line, on a day it did something |
| The cap or the reserve held someone back | the existing WARN alert `cv-resurface-cap-reached`, once a day (no new alert key, no new exit code) |

The cap of `CV_RESURFACE_MAX_PER_DAY` (40 a London day, a design default) and the reserve `CV_RESURFACE_MIN_CREDITS` (1000, a design default) apply to every Caterer look, the CV rule's and the role scope's together. A Reed legacy look is NOT counted against the 40: the owner asked for the old Reed rows to be swept naturally, bounded by the existing Reed daily view budget and run limits, and 40 a day would take 18 months for 21,915 rows. What bounds it: a page is 25 profiles; a run screens at most `ceil(cv-limit * 10 / 25)` pages (200 profiles at the default limit of 20) and stops at `cv-limit` approvals; the run limit is lowered to the views left today; Phase 2 holds a claimed look back when no view is left.

One consequence to know (K-RL4): the sweeps of the territories now SCREEN the old Reed rows they meet instead of skipping them, so the number of Jev calls of a Reed run rises (up to 8 batches of 25 per run at the default limits, where it was one or two). Each call is a few cents at most. The code bound on this is `ROLE_SCOPE_REED_MAX_PER_RUN` (default 100 people of unrecorded role per run, a design default; the rest are met by a later run and nothing is recorded for them; `0` lets none through), and a run that is still going at minute 56 of its tick is ended cleanly by the alert `tick-hard-cap` (K-RL4): the real timing of a batch is not known offline.

## 7. What the owner can change

| Setting (profile `.env`, `hermes -p resourcer config set ...`) | Default | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `ROLE_SCOPE_LEGACY` | `on` | `off` restores the old skip for people whose role was never recorded; the new per-title record of Reed rejections stays. Independent of `CV_SCREEN` and `CV_RESURFACE` |
| `ROLE_SCOPE_MIN_AGE_DAYS` | `14` | the age of a Caterer person whose role was never recorded before they are looked at (never below 8) |
| `ROLE_SCOPE_REED_MAX_PER_RUN` | `100` | the most Reed people of unrecorded role that one Reed run lets through to screening; `0` lets none through |
| `CV_RESURFACE_MAX_PER_DAY`, `CV_RESURFACE_MIN_CREDITS` | `40`, `1000` | the cap and the reserve of every Caterer look; `0` for the cap stops the Caterer looks |

No restart is needed. To undo what the role scope recorded: nothing needs undoing (a row `resurface:*`, `reed:snippet` or `reed:approved` is an ordinary row of `candidate_rejections`; the previous release never reads a `reed_id` row of that table and treats a `caterer_id` row as a rejection for that title); to let one person be screened for one title again, delete that row.

## 8. What the real database holds at cutover, and what the rule does with it

Counts only (the data are not in this repository). Caterer: 34,430 rows, 17,012 unlocked, 15,675 of those in Zoho; 1,337 unlocked and never in Zoho. Of those 1,337: 214 have a rejection row (a rejection of a real title binds that title only, so they are eligible for any other title once old: K-RL3, resolved) and 1,123 have none: 956 with `created_at` empty and 167 with `created_at` between 2026-08-24 and 2026-09-29. The 956 are old and eligible the first time they come up in a Caterer search; those of the 167 and of the 214 who are younger than the minimum age wait until they pass it. Reed: 30,916 rows, 9,001 in Zoho, 21,915 seen-only (profiles that screening rejected, and a few approved people whose push never happened). Every one is eligible, lazily, the first time a Reed search meets the profile; there is no mass rewrite, and the sweep is the natural one of the territories (section 6). Two numbers cannot be known offline: how many of the 1,123 and the 21,915 come up in a search at all, and what the platforms charge (K-RL1).

## 11. Compared with the previous release

The method is `bash tests/e2e-identity.sh <tree of the previous release>`: five worlds (four to compare, one control) are run on the tree of commit `bc3e750` (a pristine copy) and on this tree, everything the pipeline leaves behind is normalised into one JSON file per tree (the Phase 2 results files, the queue statistics, the run status numbers, the `candidates`, `candidate_rejections` and `run_results` tables, every Zoho call, the screening decisions, the alerts, the Reed profile views, the counter files) and the files are compared. The worlds: the standard Caterer world of scenario 2 with the shipped defaults; the same with `ROLE_SCOPE_LEGACY=off`; Caterer plus Reed (some Reed cards rejected by the screening) with nothing legacy in the database; and the same with the leftovers of the old system (Caterer unlocked and never pushed, Reed seen and never pushed) and `ROLE_SCOPE_LEGACY=off`. The only thing taken out of the comparison, and counted instead, is the per-title ledger of Reed (`reed:snippet`, `reed:approved`), which the previous release does not write and which has no switch. RESULT (2026-10-01, Linux, Node 22.22.1): the four worlds are IDENTICAL byte for byte on the two trees (132,348, 130,907, 129,957 and 129,957 bytes compared; the per-title Reed ledger rows are 8 and 5 in the two Reed worlds on this tree and 0 on the previous one, counted and not compared). A fifth world, the control (the same leftovers with the role scope ON), is DIFFERENT, as it must be: the comparison would see a change. Two counters of the decision cache of the CV stage (`cached`, `jevCalls`) are left out of the comparison because they differ between two runs of the SAME tree (they depend on which of the concurrent reviewers asked first); everything else, including the Phase 2 results files and every Zoho call, is compared. The same worlds with the role scope on and off in one tree are compared by scenario 19 (test 19.7), which is a repository test; the comparison with the previous release is not (it needs that tree).
